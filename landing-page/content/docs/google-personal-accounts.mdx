By default, your Google consent screen may be **Internal**. Internal only lets accounts in your Google Workspace connect, and anyone else gets an `org_internal` error. Personal `@gmail.com` accounts are outside every Workspace, so they need **External**.

This page assumes you've already added the Google provider. If not, start with [Google](/docs/providers/google#connect-accounts).

## 1. Switch the audience to External

1. Open the [Audience page](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/audience) of the Cloud project that holds your Gateway's Google client.
2. Under **User type**, select **Make external**, then **Testing**.

Your existing client ID and secret keep working. There's nothing to change in EveryApp.

## 2. Recommended: stay in Testing and add test users

In Testing, only the people you list can connect. That's usually exactly what you want for a team.

1. On the same [Audience page](https://console.cloud.google.com/auth/audience), under **Test users**, select **Add users**.
2. Enter each person's Google address, `@gmail.com` or Workspace, and select **Save**. You can add up to **100**.

Once you're External, add _everyone_ who connects, including people in your own Workspace. Anyone not on the list is refused.

When they connect, people see a **"Google hasn't verified this app"** screen. They select **Continue**. That's expected for an app you made for yourself.

### Connections expire every 7 days

In Testing, Google ends every connection 7 days after the person approved it. The only exception is connections that ask for nothing beyond name, email and profile, and Gmail always asks for more. Here's what that means:

- After 7 days, apps can no longer reach that person's Gmail. Background work stops too: OpenMail's sync and draft writing pause.
- The person sees **Reconnect** in the app, and in the account menu under **Connected accounts**. Selecting it and approving Google's screen again starts a new 7 days. Nothing is lost: apps pick up where they left off.
- There's no way to extend this in Testing. If weekly reconnects are too much, see the next section.

## 3. The other option: publish and get verified

Gmail's read and modify scopes (`gmail.readonly`, `gmail.modify`, `gmail.compose`) are **restricted** scopes, and `gmail.send` is **sensitive**. To let anyone connect without test users and without expiry, you need to:

- **Publish the app** (Audience → **Publish app**). Until Google verifies it, people still see the unverified-app screen, and the project can only ever have 100 users in total. That cap can't be reset.
- **Pass Google's verification.** You need:
  - a home page and a privacy policy on a domain you own;
  - a demo video of the consent flow;
  - a written reason for each scope.

  Review takes weeks, by email.

- **Pass a security assessment (CASA) every year**, because restricted Gmail data reaches your servers. A third-party assessor does it. Google quotes roughly $500 to $4,500 a year, depending on the app.

Most self-hosted teams don't want this. Stay in Testing unless you need more than 100 people or can't live with weekly reconnects.

If everyone is in one Google Workspace, keeping the audience **Internal** avoids all of this. Internal needs no test users, no verification and no 7-day expiry.
