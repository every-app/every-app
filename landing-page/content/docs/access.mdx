---
title: Sign in through Cloudflare Access
description: Put Cloudflare Access in front of your Gateway, so people sign in at Cloudflare before they reach it.
---

Cloudflare Access is a second sign-in in front of your Gateway and its apps. Nobody reaches them until Access knows who they are: by their Cloudflare account or a code emailed to them. It's on by default and uses Cloudflare's free Zero Trust plan, which covers 50 people.

## Turn it on

`everyapp setup` turns it on once your owner account exists. At a terminal it asks first, Yes by default; in a script or an agent run it goes on by itself, and stops to ask for a team name if your Cloudflare account has none. To leave it off, answer No or run `everyapp setup --no-access`. We don't recommend it, and setup remembers the choice.

If it's off, run:

```bash
everyapp access on
```

If your Cloudflare account has no Zero Trust team yet, you're asked for a team name. People sign in at `<name>.cloudflareaccess.com`, and the name can't be changed later, so pick your company's name. There's no plan to pick and nothing to pay.

Access asks your Gateway who may come in: its members and the people you've invited. Your apps' public routes (a tracking script, a webhook), the Gateway's `/health` and AI apps connected to your Gateway stay reachable without an Access login.

## Inviting and removing people

[Invite people](/docs/people) in the Gateway as usual. They can sign in through Access as soon as they're invited: nothing to rerun. Invite them with the email address Access will see.

To remove someone, [deactivate them](/docs/people#remove-someone) in the Gateway. They're signed out at once, and Access turns them away at their next Access sign-in.

## The first sign-in

Each person signs in to Access (**Sign in with Cloudflare** if they're a member of your Cloudflare account, otherwise an emailed code) and lands in the Gateway, signed in. People you invite never need a password.

Once everyone has signed in, you can turn passwords off:

```bash
everyapp secret set EMAIL_PASSWORD_SIGN_IN
```

Enter `false`. It can't lock you out: nothing changes until an owner has signed in through Access.

## When to run it again

Run `everyapp access on` again after you deploy an app with new public routes (the deploy tells you), or after you change Access by hand in the Cloudflare dashboard. It only changes what differs.

## Check it, or turn it off

```bash
everyapp access show
everyapp access off
```

`show` lists the team, its sign-in methods, the two Access applications and the Gateway's settings; `everyapp status` says whether Access is on in one line. `off` asks first, then the Gateway's own sign-in stands alone again. The Zero Trust team stays in your Cloudflare account.

## Good to know

- An Access sign-in lasts 30 days.
- Your Gateway's own sign-in page can also offer Continue with Google, Microsoft, GitHub and others, with Access on or off. [Sign in with Google and others](/docs/sign-in)
