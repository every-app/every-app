## Where it runs

Everything runs in your Cloudflare account. The Gateway and each app are separate Cloudflare Workers, each with its own database.

The Gateway is at an address you choose, like `apps.yourcompany.com`. Each app is one level below it, like `todo.apps.yourcompany.com`.

## How apps stay private

Apps have no public address of their own. Every visit goes through the Gateway first.

When someone opens `todo.apps.yourcompany.com`:

1. The Gateway checks that they're signed in and allowed to open the todo app. If not, the app never sees the request.
2. It passes the request to the app inside Cloudflare, with a signed note saying who they are. The note works only for that app, for about two minutes.
3. The app checks the note and shows that person their data.

So even if an app has a bug, only people you've given access to can reach it. An app can open specific routes to the public, like a webhook, and the deploy lists them.

Setup also puts [Cloudflare Access](/docs/access) in front of everything: a second sign-in, at Cloudflare. If it's off, run `everyapp access on` before you invite people.

## Your data

Each app has its own database, so one app can't read another's data. Removing one app never touches another.

A deploy saves a restore point before it changes a database. [Undo a database change](/docs/update#undo-a-database-change)

Nothing is sent to us. The CLI and the Gateway have no telemetry. They talk to Cloudflare, to GitHub when you install a published app, and to the services you connect. This website counts page views with analytics we host ourselves: [Privacy](/privacy).

## Keys

Keys you share between apps, like an AI key, stay on the Gateway. An app asks the Gateway to make the call and never holds the key. An app learns who is signed in, never their password.

## Who can change things

Anyone who can sign in to your Cloudflare account can deploy apps and change the Gateway. Give that access only to people you fully trust. To let someone deploy, add them to your Cloudflare account. To stop them, remove them.

## What it costs

| What                            | Cost                                                 |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Cloudflare's Workers Paid plan  | $5 a month, then usage                               |
| A `.com` domain                 | About $10 a year                                     |
| A `.dev` or `.app` domain       | $1 for the first year                                |
| An AI key, for apps that use AI | Billed by use by OpenAI or OpenRouter, or Workers AI |
| A DataForSEO key, for OpenSEO   | Pay as you go, billed by DataForSEO                  |
| EveryApp                        | Free and open source                                 |

The $5 covers 10 million requests a month, far more than a few apps for a small team use. Past that, Cloudflare bills by use ([pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/)). You can cap what your apps spend on AI and other keys. [Usage and caps](/docs/usage)

## What it adds to your Cloudflare account

The Gateway adds one Worker, one D1 database and one KV store, all named `every-app-gateway`, plus a DNS record for its address. Each app adds its own Worker (named `every-...`), its databases and its address. Cloudflare Access and email add only what you turn on.

EveryApp never touches your other Workers, DNS records or websites.

It signs you in to Cloudflare and uploads apps with `cf`, Cloudflare's command line tool and the successor to Wrangler. `cf` comes with everyapp.

## Limits today

- EveryApp is alpha software. Found a security hole? [Report it privately](https://github.com/every-app/every-app/security/advisories/new).
- You update apps from the terminal.
- When you remove someone, it can take up to two minutes before every app stops letting them in.
- The Gateway can't move to a different address. To change it, [remove everything](/docs/update#remove-everything) and set it up again.
- A monthly cap counts dollars only where the provider reports a price (OpenRouter, DataForSEO), and can stop an app a few calls late. [Usage and caps](/docs/usage)
