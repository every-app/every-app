# Teams: today's roles and access, the gaps a company hits, and the first slice

Written 2026-10-23 for the launch on Monday 2026-10-27. Six readers (identity and roles, app access and MCP, usage attribution, Cloudflare Access and IdPs, audit and security boundaries, hosted shape and research) each read the checkout at `main` (`bc3fe476`, then `c0f916ad` with #379 merged) and produced facts with file paths; three rankers scored the gaps for the launch persona and for a 20 to 200 person company, and a judge settled the ranking. This doc records what they found, what the first slice builds this week, and what Ben has to decide. The citations were re-checked on 2026-10-24 against `main` at `5ef30882` (#381 and the threat model merged), and the line numbers are as of that commit. Paths are in this repository unless marked.

The launch persona is a founder or a 3 to 4 person startup that points Claude and other agents at its own apps and pays one provider through the gateway's shared key. The company is a 20 to 200 person team with an IT or security lead, an IdP, and someone who reconciles the provider invoice.

## 0. Summary

**What a company needs that the gateway does not have yet.** A way to see and bound what its apps and members spend through the shared provider key. A way to grant apps to a team rather than person by person. A trail of what agents did, readable without a Cloudflare login. Credentials an admin can see and revoke one at a time. Offboarding that is complete on the hosted gateway, where admins cannot deactivate. A second owner. Roles finer than owner, admin and member, inside apps as well as at the gateway. Groups that follow the IdP. None of these exist today; each has a workaround in section 2.

**What the first slice is.** Counting and a view (usage-1 #381, usage-2 #382: `usage_daily`, `usage_monthly`, Admin → Usage), opt-in per-app monthly caps (caps #416: `org_app_settings`, merged 2026-10-24 as `01dcc5c8`), and groups with app grants (groups-1 #384 merged 2026-10-24 as `94a670a4`, groups-2 #385 as `878bae6a`). Caps are null by default because of Ben's 2026-10-16 ruling that limits are a later feature and must not be on by default; they are also what the hosted preview is waiting on, since it stays invite-only because nothing bounds an organization's spend (`docs/security-model.md:238-244`). The caps and groups PRs were held until Ben's 2026-10-24 rule (merge everything green, security boundaries included) because one adds a refusal path on provider calls and the other changes the app access check at seven sites. The judge's swap, an Admin → Activity page over `mcp_tool_calls` (#434, section 4.5), was built in the same week as a fourth, additive item and merged 2026-10-24 as `4451b350`.

**What the judge would swap.** The brief named groups as the third pick; the ranking puts an Agent activity page over `mcp_tool_calls` ahead of it for launch (one day, read-only, no migration, answers "what did my agent do" and "why is delete_report missing"; it was built this week as #434 and merged 2026-10-24 as `4451b350` under usage-2's rule, as additive admin UI with no access-check change, which decision 13 asks Ben to confirm after the fact). groups-1 and groups-2 merged 2026-10-24 under Ben's rule (section 4.3); the two shape decisions (a level on the grant, an IdP source on the group) stay open as additive columns.

**What is premature.** Anything that changes the identity contract every app reads (per-app roles, read-only members, per-token limits), the ToolBus client behaviour (approvals for destructive tools), or an undecided carrier (IdP groups); operator levers with no tenant value this week (organization suspension, who may connect integrations, a gateway-side deploy role on self-host). The shapes should be decided now so groups-2 and the token policy are built once.

## 1. Today's model

### 1.1 Organizations

A self-hosted gateway has one organization. The setup claim creates exactly one owner for the whole install (`hasAnyOwnerMembership` is global; `ALREADY_CLAIMED` afterwards), names the organization after the owner and adopts any pre-deployed apps nobody can open (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/organization/owner-membership.ts:10-19`, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/SetupService.ts:105-135, 166-212`). Nobody creates, renames, leaves or deletes an organization over HTTP: Better Auth's organization endpoints and the admin plugin are in `disabledPaths` and `allowUserToCreateOrganization` is false (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/config.ts:243-286, 469`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/serverFunctions/organizations.ts:14-38`). Organizations come from two places only: the setup claim (Better Auth's `createOrganization` as a system action, or the adoption of an organization awaiting an owner, whose rename is the one UPDATE of the table, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/repositories/OrganizationRepository.ts:86-90`) and, on hosted, the sign-up hook's `createPersonalOrganization` (`personal-organization.ts:45`). The only deletes are those two paths' own rollbacks when a later step fails (`OrganizationRepository.ts:133-136` from `SetupService.ts:227`; `personal-organization.ts:65`).

The hosted gateway (`platformPolicy.hosted`, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/platform-policy.ts:187-213`) sets `openSignUp`, `personalOrgs`, `requireEmailVerification`, `accessSignIn: false`, `sharedApps`, and `tenantAdminUserActions: false`. Every sign-up without an invite link gets a personal organization named after the person, as its owner, marked by `metadata.personalFor` (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/organization/personal-organization.ts:1-10, 36-73`); an invitee gets none and joins only the inviting organization (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/config.ts:193-215`). A company on hosted is one person's personal organization grown by invitations, with no rename path. Every new session starts in the person's personal organization when they have one (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/organization/orgContext.ts:132-175`). The platform organization is the setup claim's first owner's organization, named "Every App"; it owns the shared first-party apps, which every member of every active organization can open with no access row (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/AppService.ts:20-66`; `docs/security-model.md` "Organizations and Shared Apps").

`organizations.status` is `active` or `suspended`; every reader honours it (middleware, perimeter, deploy auth, MCP) and nothing in the gateway or CLI writes `suspended`. Suspension is a manual D1 edit (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/db/auth.schema.ts:116`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/organization/orgContext.ts:184-206`). Role and membership changes reach other isolates within 30 seconds (per-isolate caches, `orgContext.ts:16-17, 35-42, 73-77`).

### 1.2 Roles

Roles are exactly `owner`, `admin` and `member`; a comma-separated role string resolves to the highest; any other value resolves to no role (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/org-roles.ts:1-40`). Authorization is a chain of four middlewares: `authMiddleware` (session, not banned), `organizationMemberMiddleware` (membership resolves, organization active), `organizationAdminMiddleware` (owner or admin), `organizationOwnerMiddleware` (owner) (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/middleware/auth.ts:21-116`). The invite dialog describes them as "Member: Opens the apps they are given; Admin: Also manages users and apps" (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/client/components/admin/InviteUserModal.tsx:29-37`).

### 1.3 App access

Access to an organization's own app is one `user_app_access` row per (organization, user, app row) with a unique index; `apps.is_default` is "Available to everyone"; there is no column for a role or a level on the grant (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/db/apps.schema.ts:66-68, 93-128`). The perimeter decides entry in `hasAppAccess`: a shared app opens for any live member of an active organization; an organization's own app needs the row (`hasAccessByUserAndAppSlug`, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/repositories/AppAccessRepository.ts:96-116`); a browser session in another of the person's organizations is re-resolved as that membership; a PAT bound to a different app is refused (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/perimeter/betterAuthAuthenticator.ts:78-92, 174-213`).

The check sites, each reading the row today: the perimeter (`hasAccessByUserAndAppSlug`), the launcher (`AppAccessService.getAppsForUser` → `findAllByUserId`, plus every shared app on hosted), the admin modal (`findAllByAppRowId`), the members capability (`OrganizationMembersRepository.listAppMembers`, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/repositories/OrganizationMembersRepository.ts:45-92`), the MCP catalogs (`AppToolRepository.accessFor` and `reachableApps`, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/repositories/AppToolRepository.ts:182-291`), the access counts (`AppRepository.findAllWithAccessCounts`) and `findAllWithoutAccess` (the setup claim's adoption).

A new app is private to the deployer plus the organization's owners and admins; a manifest with `access: "everyone"` sets `is_default` and grants every member; redeploys never change access (`packages/perimeter/src/registry/registration.ts:405-440`; the hosted deploy API applies the same rule, `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/deploy/registry.ts:223-242`). Turning `is_default` on grants every current member and every future one on sign-up, invitation acceptance and Access auto-join; turning it off keeps existing grants (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/AppService.ts:69-106`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/cloudflare-access/membership.ts:181-188, 233-250`). The Manage Access modal is a full-list set that never revokes the acting user and refuses shared apps with `SHARED_APP_ACCESS` (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/AppAccessService.ts:50-57, 90-161`). Granting or revoking access writes no log line (grep of `AppAccessService.ts` for `logEvent` finds none); `granted_by` on the row is the only record.

### 1.4 Credentials

**Browser sessions** are Better Auth sessions, always read and write on the perimeter and on `/mcp`; the gateway's read-only concept exists only for machine credentials (`docs/security-model.md:539-542`; `packages/perimeter/src/gateway.ts:431-446`).

**Personal access tokens** (`user_access_tokens`): any member creates their own over `createUserToken` (`organizationMemberMiddleware`, no role check). No scopes becomes `*`; accepted scopes match `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9:_-]{0,63}$`, up to 20, `provider:` refused, `deploy` only alone and only where the gateway takes third-party deploys. On `/mcp`, `*` or `mcp:write` is write, `mcp:read` is read, anything else is refused with `token_scope`. An MCP-only token is refused on every app route except `/mcp` (`packages/perimeter/src/session.ts:25-37`). The default lifetime is 90 days (`DEFAULT_TTL_MS`), 30 for deploy keys (`DEPLOY_KEY_TTL_MS`), 1 day to 1 year allowed (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/UserPatService.ts:20-25, 51-109`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/schemas/user-token.ts:15-27`). The brief's "30-day default" is the deploy key's. The dialog offers only "access" (sent without scopes, so `*`) or "deploy key"; nothing mints `mcp:read` (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/client/components/admin/CreateUserTokenModal.tsx:36-40, 105-120`). Tokens are listed and revoked per user only; the only page is under the owner/admin-gated `/admin` layout, flagged by a TODO (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/routes/_app/admin/tokens.tsx:83`).

**MCP OAuth connections** (`mcp_connections`) record the consent choice: `app_scope` all (including future apps) or selected, `access` read or write; effective access is the connection's choice capped by the token's scope (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/db/mcp.schema.ts:87-142`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/mcp-auth/validate.ts:192-199`). Users list and revoke their own under `/settings/connections`; no admin surface lists another member's.

**The Access caller**: a Cloudflare Access assertion on `/mcp` (Managed OAuth) resolves to the member linked to that email, with `write` access to every app they can use, no connection row, and the organization from the sole membership or the latest live session, else `organization_unresolved` (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/mcp-auth/access-caller.ts:27-33, 80-105, 132-144`).

**ToolBus policy v1**: destructive tools are hidden from everyone (no approval path), write tools from read-only callers, a tool without a risk counts as write; the organization override parameter exists in `policy.ts` but `execute.ts` never passes it (`packages/toolbus/src/policy.ts:1-39`; `packages/toolbus/src/execute.ts:158-166`).

Every credential re-checks the ban, the membership and the organization status on each request, so a deactivated person is refused everywhere at once (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/middleware/auth.ts:34-37`; `betterAuthAuthenticator.ts:55-76, 114-115, 158`; `deploy/auth.ts:55-61`; `mcp-auth/validate.ts:124-125`; `access-caller.ts:113-114`).

### 1.5 Cloudflare Access: membership and offboarding

Access only proves an email; membership stays the gateway's. An Access identity gets a session for (a) an existing non-deactivated member, (b) a pending unexpired invitation for that email (accepted on the spot), (c) a domain in `CLOUDFLARE_ACCESS_AUTO_JOIN_DOMAINS` (exact match, several allowed), or (d) anyone Access admits when `CLOUDFLARE_ACCESS_OUTER_RING_AUTO_JOIN=everyone` (outer ring only). Everyone else is `ACCESS_NOT_INVITED` (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/cloudflare-access/membership.ts:112-123, 145-155, 163-253`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/cloudflare-access/config.ts:249-286`). Auto-joiners always become plain members of the first owner's organization with its default apps; no IdP attribute influences the role or the grants.

JWT verification accepts RS256 with a `kid` in the team JWKS, the configured issuer and audience, an `exp`, a non-empty email with `email_verified` not false; it returns email, name and the raw claims, and nothing reads `groups`, `custom` or `sub` (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/cloudflare-access/jwt.ts:220-266`; `outer-ring.ts:45-53`; `oidc-provider.ts:79-98`). OIDC sign-in requests only `openid`, `email` and `profile`, never `groups` (`oidc-provider.ts:17-19`). Service tokens never sign anyone in. Apps never see Access: the perimeter strips every `cf-access-*` header plus Cookie and Authorization (`packages/perimeter/src/headers.ts:18-59`). Access sign-in is self-hosted only: hosted ignores every `CLOUDFLARE_ACCESS_*` setting (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/auth/cloudflare-access/index.ts:19-42`).

`everyapp access on|off|show` (#379, merged 2026-10-23) keeps one policy "Every App gateway members" with a per-person `email` rule for every non-deactivated user and pending invitation, plus `--email` and `--email-domain` rules; it only adds, never removes, and keeps rules it did not write; new applications get a 720h session; deploys and deletes sync only the apps application's destinations, so `access on` must be re-run after each invitation (`packages/cli/src/commands/access/index.ts:91-95, 210-234, 313-338`; `packages/cli/src/lib/access/plan.ts:267-333`; TODO.md "Cloudflare Access from the CLI").

**Offboarding is Deactivate, not Access removal.** Deactivate bans the account, deletes every session and revokes every PAT in one D1 batch, then marks every MCP connection revoked (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/repositories/UserRepository.ts:62-85`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/McpConnectionService.ts:253-301`). Taking someone out of the Access policy stops new Access sign-ins and ends browser access when the Access session lapses (720h on new applications); PATs and OAuth connections never pass through Access (`docs/runbooks/cloudflare-access.md:63-87`; `docs/security-model.md:493-501`). Delete (owner only) deactivates instead when auto-join would let the person straight back in; otherwise `removeMember` runs one batch (membership, their `user_app_access` rows, their PATs for that organization), revokes their connections, and deletes the account only when no memberships remain and `tenantAdminUserActions` holds (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/services/AdminService.ts:110-153`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/access-rejoin.ts:12-14`; `OrganizationMembersRepository.ts:171-204`). On hosted, deactivate, reactivate and reset throw `ACCOUNT_ACTIONS_UNAVAILABLE`; Delete removes the membership but leaves the person's sessions untouched (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/tenant-user-actions.ts:11-23`). What survives any gateway action: the Cloudflare login or API token; `user_app_access` rows survive deactivation, so Reactivate silently restores every app.

### 1.6 What is attributed and logged today, and what cannot be counted

The AppGateway entrypoint attributes every AI and capability call to one organization with an `orgSource` of `props` (an organization's own app), `jwt` (a shared app with a verified forwarded identity) or `header` (a shared app's background work naming `x-everyapp-org`), plus the verified user or null (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/app-gateway-entrypoint.ts:417-519`). For an organization's own app a failed identity silently becomes the app's call (`attribution: app`, no user; `resolveAttribution`, `:573-596`); for a shared app the rules are strict and refused before any upstream fetch (`caller_identity_invalid`, `caller_identity_required`, `caller_org_not_attributable`, `caller_org_mismatch`; `docs/security-model.md:215-226`).

- `ai.proxy.request`: a plain `console.info` object with organization, app, Worker, provider, `orgSource`, attribution and `userId` when attributed; emitted before the AI proxy's own refusals, so a request line does not mean a provider was called (`app-gateway-entrypoint.ts:281-290`; `ai-proxy.ts:205-275`).
- `ai.proxy.usage`: `logEvent` with provider, organization, app, route, model, status, `requestId` and token usage or null. The caller passed is only `{ organizationId, appId }`, so the line carries no user; nothing joins it to the request line (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/ai-proxy.ts:154, 322-337`; the `usage_daily` row #381 writes beside it does carry the user, section 4.1). Tokens come from a regex over the last 16 KB of the response; since #381 the tap (`response-tap.ts`) hears a reader in the gateway's own isolate cancel or the provider fail mid-body, but a browser or app Worker that stops reading on the other side of the boundary is never heard, so the call is counted when the provider answers and the tokens only when the body was read; OpenRouter's per-response `usage.cost` is not parsed; `stream_options.include_usage` is never forced (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/ai-usage.ts:11-63`).
- `integration.proxy.call`: `logEvent` with capability, caller, host, path, method, attribution, `userId`, status and `cost` when the first 2,048 bytes carry a top-level `cost` number (DataForSEO's envelope) (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/integration-proxy.ts:49-50, 240-328`).
- `email.app.send`, `members.app.list`: counts and an HMAC of the first recipient, never addresses (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/capabilities/email.ts:198-212`; `members.ts:84-90`). Mail budgets are per recipient and per gateway, never per organization (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/email/sendEmail.ts:123-194`; `platform-policy.ts:84-91`).
- `mcp_tool_calls`: the one per-call table: organization, user, credential kind and id, client, surface, app row and slug, tool, risk, decision, reason, outcome, duration, an HMAC digest of the arguments and the result's byte count; no foreign keys; pruned hourly past `MCP_AUDIT_RETENTION_DAYS` (90 by default, up to 3650); written best-effort under `waitUntil`; nothing in the UI reads it (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/db/mcp.schema.ts:144-191`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/toolbus/runtime.ts:49-79`; `apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/mcp-auth/maintenance.ts:25-27, 40-45`).
- `[research-spend]`: a `console.log` in OpenSEO's own Worker with `organizationId`, `projectId`, `totalUsd` and a per-provider spend array from OpenSEO's own credit pricing, not the gateway's cost (`~/workspaces/open-seo-everyapp/src/server/billing/researchSpend.ts:13-50`; the gateway-side note of it was in the 2026-10 hosted-platform build log, removed by #433).
- Member and invitation events (`member.role_changed`, `member.deactivated`, `invitation.created`, `access.member_joined`, ...) are `logEvent` lines with a `by` field; app access changes and token creation or revocation log nothing (`MembershipService.ts:67-72, 115-119`; grep of `AppAccessService.ts`, `UserPatService.ts`, `serverFunctions/userTokens.ts`, `serverFunctions/admin.ts` for `logEvent`: none).

All log lines go to Workers Logs on the operator's Cloudflare account through `logEvent` (one JSON line, keys matching token, secret, password or code redacted, depth 4; `packages/perimeter/src/log.ts:1-57`); Cloudflare keeps them 3 days on Workers Free and 7 on Workers Paid, the longest it offers (the pricing and limits tables at https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/observability/logs/workers-logs/, fetched 2026-10-23; the repo records nothing about retention, though the hosted research assumed the 7 days), and the repo configures no Logpush, OpenTelemetry export or Tail Worker. The lines are read in the Cloudflare dashboard by anyone with observability access to the account, and redaction is by key name only, so there is no log-reader role of the gateway's own, distinct from a gateway admin.

**What cannot be counted today**, from the code as it stood at `c0f916ad` (#381, merged 2026-10-24, adds the per-member counters of section 4.1 and counts an abandoned stream as a call with no tokens; the rest stands): any per-member token total (no user on the usage line); any AI cost (no price table, OpenRouter's cost dropped, Workers AI reports none); tokens for streamed OpenAI chat without `include_usage`, for any abandoned stream, and for image or audio routes; a cached-versus-fresh split; cost for Context.dev, the OAuth-client providers, members and email; email volume per organization or app; and anything once the Workers Logs window has passed. Nothing is refused on volume in any mode: #316 removed the daily caps and their 429 entirely (the ruling as `docs/hosted-vision.md:44` records it; `docs/security-model.md:238-244`; the build log that carried the walk-back went with #433).

### 1.7 Operator authority

On a self-hosted gateway, deploying an organization's own app needs no gateway role. The CLI writes the registry in D1 under the operator's Cloudflare login or API token, and the gateway has no deploy API in that mode; only shared apps require the login's verified email to be an owner or admin of the platform organization (`SHARED_APP_FORBIDDEN`), and an API token (no email) bypasses the check as the account's own authority (`packages/cli/src/lib/gateway/registry.ts:12-16, 384-428`; `docs/security-model.md:189-197, 246-293`). Revocation is Cloudflare's alone: removing or deactivating a gateway member does not stop someone who still holds the Cloudflare login or an API token with D1 write, and there is no deploy event log, only `deployed_by_actor` overwritten on the app row. Reading the logs is Cloudflare's too: Workers Logs open to anyone with observability access on the account, with key-name redaction only (section 1.6), so a log reader and a deployer are both Cloudflare account roles and the docs should say which to give each (a read-only account role for the reader; Workers and D1 write, or a scoped API token, for the deployer). The public docs say so in one line ("Anyone who can sign in to your Cloudflare account can deploy apps and change the Gateway", `landing-page/content/docs/how-it-works.mdx:26-30`). Hosted already closes this: deploy keys and browser installs require a live owner or admin (`apps/every-app-gateway/src/server/deploy/auth.ts:29-87`; `install.ts:59-104`).

## 2. The gaps a 20 to 200 person company hits

One subsection per item in the ranking, in ranked order. Effort is in agent-days of what remains. The boundaries these gaps sit on are the threat model's (`docs/threat-model.md`, #413: B2 the control plane, B4 AI and capabilities, B5 `/mcp`, B6 Access, B7 deploy authority), which pins each with an invariant and a test, while `docs/security-model.md` describes the mechanisms. Which provider scopes an app may ask for is the OAuth-scopes design's (`docs/design/2026-10-oauth-scopes.md`, #393), not this doc's. ### 2.19 Groups mapped to IdP groups through Cloudflare Access

**What breaks today.** Nothing reads groups from Access: `jwt.ts` returns email and name only, OIDC sign-in requests `openid`, `email` and `profile`, and no code calls `get-identity`. Every SSO joiner is a plain member with the default apps and must be upgraded and granted apps by hand.

**What the Access JWT carries.** The application token (`Cf-Access-Jwt-Assertion`) carries `aud`, `email`, `exp`, `iat`, `nbf`, `iss`, `type` (app or org), `identity_nonce`, `sub` and `country`. Custom SAML attributes and OIDC claims are included in a `custom` claim on a best-effort basis and trimmed once the serialized claim exceeds about 1 KB (groups are usually the largest part); groups appear only if configured as a custom attribute or claim named `groups`. The full identity including all groups comes from `https://<team>.cloudflareaccess.com/cdn-cgi/access/get-identity` with the `CF_Authorization` cookie. A service token's JWT has `common_name` and an empty `sub` (Cloudflare docs, "Application token", https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/identity/authorization-cookie/application-token/). On the Access for SaaS OIDC path (the gateway's "Continue with Company SSO" mode) Access supports the scopes `openid`, `email`, `profile` and `groups`, a group filter regex, and custom claims through the issuer's token, JWKS and userinfo endpoints (https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/applications/http-apps/saas-apps/generic-oidc-saas/). Access policies can select on "Identity provider group" (Entra ID, GitHub, Google, Okta, or SCIM-provisioned), "SAML Group" and "OIDC Claim"; identity selectors are checked at login only; the API rule shapes are `okta`, `azureAD`, `gsuite`, `saml`, `oidc` and the Access rule group `group` (https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/access-controls/policies/; https://developers.cloudflare.com/api/resources/zero_trust/subresources/access/subresources/policies/methods/create/). SCIM 2.0 from any IdP revokes Access sessions on deprovision and can revoke on group change (https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/team-and-resources/users/scim/). Zero Trust Free stops at 50 users; pay-as-you-go is $7 per user per month (https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/team-and-resources/users/seat-management/).

**Today's workaround.** Mark company-wide apps Available to everyone and grant the rest per user; or, by hand in Zero Trust, add a per-app-host Access application with an Identity provider group policy and put its AUD in `CLOUDFLARE_ACCESS_APPS_AUD` (the policy keeps rules `access on` did not write).

**What to build.** After groups-1: an `idp_group` source on a group; the `groups` scope on the OIDC path (or a server-side call to `get-identity` at sign-in; the outer-ring `custom` claim is unreliable past 1 KB); `group_members` reconciled on each Access sign-in with the diff logged; `access on --group` writing the IdP rule shapes. The carrier must be decided first.

**Effort.** 3.5. **Hosted fit.** None; hosted has no Access. **Premature?** Yes: self-host only, needs groups-1 and the OIDC automation that does not exist, a wrong mapping grants apps silently, and no launch customer runs an IdP against the gateway yet. Decide the carrier now so groups-2 leaves room for the column.

### 4.1 Usage counters (usage-1, #381, merged 2026-10-24 as `5ef30882`) and Admin → Usage (usage-2, #382, merged 2026-10-24 as `177830b7`)

**Data model** (`src/db/usage.schema.ts`, migration `0003_usage_counters.sql`, additive):

- `usage_daily`: `organization_id` (the attributed organization), `app_row_id` (`apps.id`; for a shared app the platform organization's row), `user_id` (`''` when the call was the app's own: background work or an unverified identity), `kind` (`ai` | `capability`), `target` (AI provider id such as `openai`, `openrouter`, `workers-ai`, or the capability id such as `dataforseo`, `context`, `email`, `members`), `day` (UTC `YYYY-MM-DD`), `calls`, `failures` (upstream status >= 400), `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cost_micros` (provider-reported cost in USD millionths, DataForSEO's `cost`), `updated_at`. PRIMARY KEY (`organization_id`, `app_row_id`, `user_id`, `kind`, `target`, `day`); indexes (`organization_id`, `day`), (`app_row_id`, `day`). No foreign keys: the trail outlives the user and the app, like `mcp_tool_calls`.
- `usage_monthly`: `organization_id`, `app_row_id`, `month` (`YYYY-MM`), `calls`, `failures`, `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cost_micros`, `updated_at`. PRIMARY KEY (`organization_id`, `app_row_id`, `month`). The per-app monthly total the cap reads in one row.
- `UsageRepository.record(event)` upserts both in one `db.batch` (ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE ... + delta). A `UsageEvent` (`src/server/usage-event.ts`) carries `calls: 0 | 1`: 1 for the call itself, 0 for an addition (tokens or a cost read off the body later) to a call already counted; a failure is counted for the call only, never for an addition, and an addition with nothing to add is not written. `UsageRepository.summarize({ organizationId, month })` returns by-app, by-member, by-target aggregates (row types in `src/types/usage.ts`, shared by the repository, the server function and the client); `deleteOlderThan` for the hourly maintenance (daily rows past 13 months, monthly past 25, both cutoffs from the first of the current UTC month), bounded at 1,000 rows per statement and 10 statements per table per run, so 10,000 rows of each an hour; a month of daily rows ages out over the hours after a month boundary and the `mcp.maintenance` line carries `usage_behind` while rows remain, beside `usage_daily_deleted` and `usage_monthly_deleted`.

**Hook points.** `handleAppGatewayRequest` gains optional deps `usage?: { record(event): Promise<void> }` and `waitUntil?`; `src/server.ts`'s `AppGateway` passes `UsageRepository` and `(t) => this.ctx.waitUntil(t)`. A call is counted the moment the provider answers (its headers arrive), with the status it answered, under `waitUntil` before the Response is returned: in workerd the gateway is not told when a browser or an app Worker stops reading the body afterwards, and the provider bills from the answer. `handleAuthenticatedAiProxyRequest` gains `onAnswer?` (the call, `calls` 1) and `onUsage?` (the tokens, `calls` 0, where `ai.proxy.usage` is logged, only when the body was read to the end); `handleIntegrationProxyRequest` gains `onCall?` (the call, where `integration.proxy.call` is logged; passed through `handleOAuthClientCall` too, whose report is `{ capability, status }` with no cost field) and `onCost?` (DataForSEO's `cost`, read from the head of the body, or the whole body when the head has none). The entrypoint composes attribution (`organizationId`, `app.rowId`, `user?.id ?? ''`) with the report; the attribution is set only after `admit` or the AI route's attribution succeeds, so every refusal before the upstream fetch (policy, provider not allowed, unknown background organization, invalid identity, capability not listed, provider unreachable) counts nothing. When `app.rowId` is absent (in-memory registries) nothing is recorded. An upstream 4xx or 5xx counts as a call and a failure. So a stream the person abandons, or an app stops reading, counts as one call with no tokens; a capability body the app drops counts as a call with no cost; a provider failing mid-body counts as a call, not a failure, since its status was already 2xx. `src/server/response-tap.ts` (`watchBody`) pumps the body so the taps hear a reader in the gateway's own isolate cancelling or a provider failing mid-body, and `ai.proxy.usage` then says `ended: cancelled | failed` (absent on a fully read body); that is in-isolate information only, a closed tab on everyapp.team does not produce it, and the tap docs and test names say so. A failed write is logged (`usage.record.failed`) and never fails the app's call. Never log or store prompts, bodies or keys. Allowed tool calls per app and per member are read from `mcp_tool_calls` in the same batch for the view (no new writes).

**UI** (`/admin/usage`, tab icon lucide `Activity`): a month picker (this UTC month and the 11 before), a Refresh button (a sonner toast on failure) rather than polling, three DataTables: By app (name, provider calls, failures, tokens in and out, provider cost, agent tool calls), By member (same; a "Background" row for `''`; a person who is no longer a member of the organization shows as "Former member" with their id, since names come from a join on `members` for this organization, never from the profile they keep in another one; their own organization still sees their name), By provider (kind and target, calls, tokens, cost). A removed app shows by id. Server function `getUsage({ month })` (GET, listed in the READS of `src/serverFunctions/methods.test.ts`) under `organizationAdminMiddleware`, month validated as `YYYY-MM`, always the session's organization. Empty state: "Calls apps make through the gateway (AI providers, capabilities and agent tool calls) are counted from now on. Earlier calls were not." Thousands separators; cost as `$0.00` for none, four decimals under a dollar, two from there.

**Defaults taken** (checked against the diffs of #381 and #382): only provider calls are counted (`api_key` capabilities and OAuth-client token calls); the gateway's own `email` and `members` services log their own events and are not counted in this slice, the `target` column takes them when wanted. Tokens only when the provider reports usage; a call with none still counts as a call. OAuth-client token calls count with 0 micros. `summarize` joins names at read time with left joins so a deleted app or user still shows by id; rows are sorted by provider calls then tool calls, server side. Retention 13 and 25 months counted from the first of the current month, both deletes in the hourly cron with one summary field each. The month list is the last 12 UTC months. Tool calls are shown beside provider totals, not added to them. Tests: the summarize-shape tests sit in usage-1 (`usage-repository.sqlite.test.ts`), since `summarize` ships there; usage-2 adds the admin-gate and validator tests for the server function and the formatting tests.

### 4.2 Per-app monthly caps (caps, #416, merged 2026-10-24 as `01dcc5c8`)

- `org_app_settings` (`src/db/org-app-settings.schema.ts`, migration `0006_org_app_settings.sql`, journal idx 6, regenerated on top of `0005_groups` (#384) once the secrets migration `0004_app_secret_names` (#383) had taken 0004, the SQL byte-identical at every renumbering; not in `SHIPPED_MIGRATIONS` until the live gateway runs it): `organization_id` FK organizations cascade, `app_row_id` FK `apps(id)` cascade (a plain FK: a shared app's row belongs to the platform organization, so the composite FK the other tables use cannot hold), `monthly_call_cap` integer NULL (null = unlimited, the default), `updated_at`, `updated_by` FK users set null. PRIMARY KEY (`organization_id`, `app_row_id`). Clearing a cap deletes the row rather than writing null, since the table has no other column yet.
- What a cap counts is exactly what `usage_monthly` counts: AI provider calls and provider capability calls (`api_key` and OAuth-client token calls), error answers included. The gateway's own services (`email`, `members`) are neither counted nor capped; the entrypoint tells them apart with `parseIntegrationProxyPath` (kind `service`) before `admit` runs, and an app with no registry row is never capped since it is never counted.
- Check (`src/server/app-cap.ts`, `checkMonthlyCap`) in the entrypoint after attribution, before any upstream fetch, for AI and capability calls alike: caps cached per isolate (30 s when set, 5 s when none, 500 entries; `clearAppCallerCacheForTests` clears it too, and `setAppMonthlyCap` clears the one (organization, app) entry, so a cap binds at once in the isolate that served the Admin request and within seconds elsewhere); when a cap is set, read the one `usage_monthly` row for (organization, app, current UTC month); if `calls >= cap` → **429, code `app_monthly_cap_exceeded`**, message "<app> has reached its monthly cap of <cap> provider calls for this organization; an owner or admin raises it in Admin → Usage.", `Retry-After` = seconds until the UTC month ends (up to 31 days, at least 1); AI routes answer in OpenAI's error shape through `aiRefusal` (which now takes headers), capability routes in the capability refusal shape with "Too Many Requests"; logged as `usage.cap_exceeded` (a `console.warn` with `organizationId`, `appId`, `appRowId`, `cap`, `calls`, `month`). A refused call is not counted. The check is soft: counts land under `waitUntil` after the provider answers, so a burst can run a few calls past the cap; a hard stop would need a counter read in the request path.
- UI: in Admin → Usage "By app", a "Monthly cap" column: "Unlimited" with a Set cap button, or the cap with a progress bar and "1,240 of 5,000" in the current month, the cap alone in a past month (the cap is on the current month's calls); a dialog sets or clears it (owner or admin). `summarize` gained a sixth batched query over `org_app_settings`, so By app lists every app the organization can call today (its own and the shared ones, never a deleted app's tombstone), in every month, zeros included: a cap can be set before the first call and is always visible to clear; a deleted app shows only in a month it spent in, muted, with no cap and no button. When nothing was counted in a month the page shows the By app table alone with the "counted from now on" note as its hint, and the full empty state only when the organization has no apps. Server function `setAppMonthlyCap({ appRowId, cap: number | null })` (`AppCapService`), whole numbers 1 to 1,000,000,000; the app must be the organization's own or a shared app (`AppRepository.findCallableById`), else `APP_NOT_FOUND`; a shared app is capped per organization on the platform organization's row; logged as `usage.cap_set` (`organizationId`, `appId`, `appRowId`, `cap`, `userId`). The UI says the unit is calls, not dollars.
- Deleting an app (`AppRepository.tombstone`, the CLI's `everyapp delete` and the perimeter's `deleteUpdate`) deletes every organization's cap on it, a shared app's included when the platform organization deletes it, like its email configuration: a revived app starts uncapped. A later hide or disable setting in `org_app_settings` would go the same way.
- Tests (`app-cap.test.ts`, `app-cap.sqlite.test.ts`, `AppCapService.sqlite.test.ts`): cap reached refuses an AI call and a capability call with 429, the seconds left in the month and no count, in both refusal shapes; under the cap passes and counts up to the cap; a null cap never reads the counter; the cache's 30 s and 5 s and the clear on set; UTC month rollover; one organization's cap on a shared app binds to that organization only; an OAuth client's token call is refused at the cap; the members list answers at the cap; a deleted app's tombstone holds no cap. No browser smoke was run (no dev server in the worktree).
- `docs/security-model.md` ("No per-organization usage limits yet" and the threat model's `cap.per-organization-limits` gap, rewritten on main by #413 after the stack was cut) needs the cap paragraph now that this merged; the wording is in the #416 body, the threat-model thread owns the file, and the edit is recorded on main as a TODO item by #426.

### 4.4 Why the slice is forward-compatible with the hosted wallet and install rows

- `usage_daily` and `usage_monthly` are keyed by `organizations.id`, which the research fixed as the Autumn customer id, never renamed (research D5), and by `apps.id`, the research's Autumn entity (`entity_id = apps.id`, attribution only; H13). A shared app's calls attribute to the customer organization, not the platform organization that owns the row, which is the tenant view Autumn needs. The research kept the raw ledger out of D1 (C5: one single-threaded database, 10 GB); the slice stores daily and monthly rollups only, and the per-event ledger moves to the OrgWallet Durable Object when the money lane lands, behind the pinned `BillingPort` (`reserve`, `settle`, `track`), with nothing renamed (the 2026-10 hosted-platform research and its final plan, removed by #433 and readable at `5ef30882` by the recipe in `docs/hosted-vision.md` section 2; the contract keeps the wallet as later work, `docs/hosted-vision.md:26-27`).
- `org_app_settings(organization_id, app_row_id)` is the install row the research reserved for per-(organization, app) caps and hide or disable (the final plan's PR 6 (f), removed by #433; `docs/hosted-vision.md:262-263` keeps `org_app_settings (org_id, app_id)` as the walk-back for hiding or capping a shared app), spelled with the repo's column convention (`user_app_access.app_row_id`, `mcp_tool_calls.app_row_id`). The plain FK on `apps(id)` is what a shared app needs. An `org_app_settings` row must never imply an Autumn entity (entities are created on the first metered call). A later `monthly_credit_cap` (the research's unit: credits per month, 1 credit = $0.001, 1.28x markup) sits beside `monthly_call_cap`; the 429 code `app_monthly_cap_exceeded` stays for both; enforcement moves to the OrgWallet DO, never to Autumn entity `usage_limits` (A5, D4).
- `cost_micros` stores raw provider-reported USD; credits are derived at display or billing time.
- UTC calendar months (`usage_monthly.month`) match Autumn only with `anchor: "utc"` on the catalog's usage limits; pin that when the catalog is written.
- Groups are gateway-local and access-only: a group is never a cap, policy or Autumn unit (entities are apps or users, never both; C9). `group_app_access` has room for a `level` column if per-app roles land, and `groups` for an `idp_group` source.
- `mcp_tool_calls` is the precedent for all of it: organization, user and app row id, no foreign keys, `(organization_id, created_at)` index, pruned by the hourly cron.

Alternative: count only when the body was read to the end, which undercounts what the provider bills.
22. 23. Usage prune bounds: 1,000 rows per statement, at most 10 statements per table per hourly run, so a month of daily rows ages out over the hours after a month boundary; `usage_behind` on the maintenance line while rows remain.
24. The cap check is soft: `calls >= cap` from the one `usage_monthly` row, counts landing under `waitUntil` after the provider answers, so a burst can run a few calls past the cap. A hard stop would need a counter read in the request path (the `rate_limit_counters` pattern). The cap counts exactly what `usage_monthly` counts, error answers included; the gateway's own `email` and `members` services are neither counted nor capped.
25. The cap's edges: cached 30 s when set, 5 s when none, 500 entries an isolate, the one (organization, app) entry cleared by `setAppMonthlyCap`; clearing a cap deletes the `org_app_settings` row; the app must be the organization's own or a shared app, else `APP_NOT_FOUND`; the 429 is OpenAI's error shape on AI routes and the capability refusal shape with "Too Many Requests" on capability routes, `Retry-After` up to 31 days; `usage.cap_exceeded` is a `console.warn` with the count and the month, `usage.cap_set` a `logEvent` with the actor.
26. By app lists every app the organization can call today, in every month, zeros included (in hosted mode every shared app for every organization; an app registered after a past month still shows there, no `created_at` filter), so a cap can be set before the first call and is always visible to clear; a capped app with no calls shows with zeros; a past month shows the cap without a progress bar; a deleted app shows only in a month it spent in, muted, with no cap. When nothing was counted, the By app table alone with the "counted from now on" hint; the full empty state only when the organization has no apps.
27. Deleting an app (Admin, CLI or the perimeter's `deleteUpdate`) deletes every organization's cap on it, a shared app's included when the platform organization deletes it, like its email configuration: a revived app starts uncapped. A later hide or disable setting in `org_app_settings` goes the same way.
